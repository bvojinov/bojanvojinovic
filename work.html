Real data problems. Practical solutions. Focus on control, ownership and trust.

Bringing structure to fragmented data environments

The problem

Multiple data sources, inconsistent definitions and unclear ownership resulted in reports that did not match. Business users were spending more time questioning data than using it.

The approach

Introduced Data Governance structure including ownership model, data definitions and validation rules. Connected business processes with data flows to ensure consistency from input to reporting.

The outcome

Improved consistency across reports, increased trust in data and reduced time spent on manual reconciliation.

Improving data quality through validation and control

The problem

Data quality issues were recurring across key business domains, with no structured way to identify, track or resolve them.

The approach

Designed and implemented validation rules, monitoring processes and issue tracking logic. Focused on making data quality visible and manageable.

The outcome

Increased transparency of data issues, faster resolution cycles and more reliable data for reporting and analysis.

Defining ownership and accountability for data

The problem

No clear ownership of data across teams led to inconsistent inputs, duplicated work and lack of accountability.

The approach

Established ownership model with clearly defined roles and responsibilities. Aligned business users with data processes and governance rules.

The outcome

Clear accountability, improved data consistency and better collaboration between business and data teams.

Data does not become reliable by itself.
It becomes reliable when someone is responsible for it.
